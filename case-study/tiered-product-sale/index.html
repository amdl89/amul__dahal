Automating a Tiered Product Sale with Shopify Flow

A self-running pricing engine that advances to the next tier the moment the current one sells out — turning every sell-out into urgency, lifting average selling price as scarcity builds, and clearing every round without deadstock.

Project Overview

This project automates a tiered product sale on a Shopify store. Each variant is released across multiple rounds — Round 1 is the original price and inventory, and each subsequent round redefines both. When a round sells out, the next round activates automatically: price changes, stock is replenished, and the storefront keeps moving.

The transition is event-driven. The Shopify Flow "variant out of stock" trigger fires the moment inventory hits zero, eliminating timer-based releases and the operational overhead of merchants pricing rounds manually.

The Challenge
  • Advance through rounds without human intervention
  • Update price and stock together — Shopify keeps them in different domains
  • Stay safe under the Admin API's idempotency rules so a retry never double-adjusts inventory

Key Strategy

Treat each round as a piece of state stored on the variant itself, then let the flow read that state at trigger-time and act on whichever tier comes next.

  • State on the variant, not in the flow

    Round prices and quantities live in variant metafields. The flow stays stateless and idempotent.

  • Event-driven transitions

    variant_out_of_stock_updated fires precisely when a tier is exhausted — no polling, no timers.

  • Two-call sequencing for atomicity

    Price (variant) is set before stock (inventory level), so customers never see new stock at the old price.

What is a Tiered Product Sale?

A tiered (or "round-based") product sale releases a SKU across a sequence of pricing windows. Each round has its own price and its own quantity. The earliest round usually carries the lowest price and the largest pool of stock; successive rounds raise the price as supply tightens.

The mechanic does three commercial jobs at once:

  • Captures the price-sensitive segment first. The cheapest tier converts customers who would otherwise stay on the fence.
  • Lifts average selling price (ASP). Later rounds harvest demand at higher margins from customers who were waiting to see if the product was "for them."
  • Generates recurring engagement events. Each tier transition becomes a moment for email, SMS, or push: "Round 2 is live — at $30 instead of $20."

The strategy aligns with how customers already behave. According to a 2024 YouGov survey cited by Shopify, 31 % of consumers across 17 international markets say they're more likely to buy clothes, shoes, or accessories carrying a "limited-edition" label — and 39 % of Gen Z shoppers say the same, the strongest response of any age group.

For inventory-constrained brands the operational benefit is just as important: pre-committing to sell each tier through before advancing eliminates deadstock risk and simplifies forecasting. The mechanic only works at scale if the transitions are automatic — which is what this flow exists to deliver.

Round 1 is the variant's default state — the price and inventory you'd already enter into Shopify. Subsequent rounds live in metafields and only activate when their predecessor sells out.

Data Model: State on the Variant

Each round's price and quantity is stored as a variant metafield in the variant namespace. Round 1 needs no metafields — it is the variant's default state. Rounds 2+ are added as needed:

Namespace.Key Type Purpose
variant.round_2_price money Price applied when Round 1 sells out
variant.round_2_quantity number_integer Stock injected for Round 2 (positive delta)
variant.round_3_price money Price applied when Round 2 sells out
variant.round_3_quantity number_integer Stock injected for Round 3

The metafields double as the flow's state machine. After a tier activates, its quantity metafield is reset to 0. The next time the trigger fires, the condition for that tier evaluates false and the flow advances to the next one. No external database, no scheduled job — the variant is the ledger.

Two Admin API Endpoints, Sequenced

Price and stock live in different parts of Shopify's data model. Price is a field on the ProductVariant; stock is a field on an InventoryLevel (the join between an InventoryItem and a Location). No single mutation spans both, so each round transition makes two calls in a fixed order.

Step 1 of 2

productVariantsBulkUpdate

Sets the variant's price for the active tier. Requires write_products scope. productId is a top-level argument because the mutation is bulk-scoped to a single product. price is a decimal string (Shopify represents money as strings to avoid float drift).

Price-first ordering matters: if stock were injected before the new price posted, customers browsing in the gap would see Round 2 inventory at the Round 1 price. 

# Send Admin API request — body
{
  "productId": "{{productVariant.product.id}}",
  "variants": [
    {
      "id": "{{productVariant.id}}",
      "price": "{{productVariant.round2Price.value.amount}}"
    }
  ]
}
Step 2 of 2

inventoryAdjustQuantities

Adds the new tier's stock as a signed delta — the API never accepts an absolute quantity, only "add this many" or "subtract this many." Because the trigger fires the instant the variant hits zero, a positive delta equal to the tier's quantity restocks the variant cleanly to that exact level.

name: "available" writes to the bucket customers actually see; reason: "correction" stamps the audit log with a recognizable cause; the locationId is required because one inventory item can have different stock levels at different warehouses. 

# Send Admin API request — body
{
  "input": {
    "reason": "correction",
    "name": "available",
    "changes": [
      {
        "delta": {{productVariant.round2Quantity.value}},
        "inventoryItemId": "{{productVariant.inventoryItem.id}}",
        "locationId": "gid://shopify/Location/<your_location>"
      }
    ]
  }
}
Idempotency note — as of API version 2026-04, inventoryAdjustQuantities requires an idempotency key on retries to prevent duplicate adjustments. Shopify Flow handles retry semantics internally, but for custom apps calling the same endpoint you'll want to derive a stable key from variant.id + tier number + the round's start timestamp.

Building the Flow

The flow is built entirely in Shopify Flow's visual builder. The variant_out_of_stock_updated trigger branches on which round still has stock to release, and each branch carries its own price update, inventory adjustment, and metafield reset.

Each tier transition is a fresh trigger event. Zeroing the tier's quantity metafield after the actions run is what makes the next out-of-stock event fall through to the next round.

The branching tree reads naturally at three rounds: a condition checks whether the next tier still has inventory to release, the true branch runs the price-and-stock pair, and the false branch falls through to the round after that. Because every action references the variant's own metafields rather than a global schedule, the workflow handles dozens of products in a single sale concurrently without any per-variant configuration.

Outcomes & Takeaways

Zero-touch tier transitions

Every round opens the moment the previous one sells out — no scheduled jobs, no merchant intervention, no risk of missed timing.

State on the variant

Each round's price and inventory live in the variant's own metafields, so the workflow handles dozens of products in a single sale concurrently with no per-variant configuration.

Safe sequencing

Price is updated before stock is injected, so a customer browsing during the transition never sees fresh inventory at last round's price.

The deeper takeaway from this build: treating the variant as the ledger turns a multi-step pricing campaign into something the storefront can run on its own. No scheduled job, no merchant logging in to flip a switch — the data on each variant carries the entire state of where the sale is, and the flow just listens for the moment to act.

That principle generalizes well beyond tiered sales: gift-with-purchase cascades, loyalty tier upgrades, segmented thank-you tagging — anywhere business state can sit on the shop's existing objects, the automation that drives it becomes shorter, safer, and easier to reason about.
